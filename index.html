    APEX Galaxy

    Every star is an athlete.

    The further right, up and forward a star sits, the better that career was on three measures: peak (Height), staying power (Length) and dominance over rivals (Separation). Seven sports, one map.

    How to read the galaxy

    Each star is one athlete's career. Where it sits comes from three numbers, one per axis. How big and bright it is comes from the GOAT score. Its color is the sport.

    Big, bright star: a top GOAT score
    Small, faint star: great, not all-time

    You're looking at the top 125 careers in each sport by default (0 stars). Tick Show everyone to see all 0 athletes in the data.

    The 3 axes

    Every season, each league has an elite tier: the best players that year (about the top 1 in 8 who played enough, fewer in big leagues). All three axes compare an athlete to that tier, so a 1960s season and a 2020s season are measured against their own competition.

    X

    Height

    How good were they at their best?

    Take their 3 best seasons. For each, measure how far above that year's elite average they were. Average the three.

    High: a peak nobody in their era could touch. Low: very good, never great.

    Y

    Length

    How long did they stay elite?

    Count the seasons they finished inside the elite tier. One great year counts once; fifteen great years count fifteen times.

    High: an iron-man career at the top. Low: a short burst.

    Z

    Separation

    How far ahead of everyone did they get?

    In each season, the gap between them and the next best player. I use their 2 biggest gaps.

    High: seasons where they weren't just #1, they lapped the field.

    Height and Separation move together a lot (the best peaks usually come with the biggest gaps). Length is the one that tells careers apart.

    The GOAT score

    The GOAT score blends the three axes: Height %, Length %, Separation % by default.

    Those weights weren't picked by feel. I tested every mix in 5% steps and kept the one that best matched published all-time lists across all seven sports: ESPN's #NBArank, The Athletic's NHL99 and NFL 100, Joe Posnanski's Baseball 100, FourFourTwo's top 100 and others. That's the Experts preset.

    Disagree? Move the sliders in Build your GOAT formula. The stars resize and the #1 at the top updates live. Presets like Longevity or Peak show how much the answer depends on what you value.

    Scores are scaled per sport pool: 50 = the average of a sport's top 100, 99 = the best career in the whole galaxy.

    How sports are compared

    Each sport gets one "season value" built from the best all-round stat available, then compared to that season's elite:

    NBAWin Shares + VORP per 82 games
    NHLPoints per 82 games (defensemen rescaled to match forwards)
    MLBHitters: OPS+ above average × plate appearances. Pitchers: ERA+ above average × innings
    NFLApproximate Value (Pro Football Reference) per 17 games
    Formula 1Points per race, modern points system applied to every era
    TennisWins weighted by tournament level, minus half-weighted losses
    SoccerLeague goals per 34 matches

    The data, and what it can't see

    NBA, NHL, MLB, F1 and tennis use every player in league history, so the elite tier is real. NFL and soccer use a smaller hand-picked pool, so they're marked with lower confidence on each athlete card.

    • No playoffs yet. Clutch performance isn't in the score.
    • Soccer is goals only. That undersells defenders, goalkeepers and playmakers.
    • Active careers are unfinished. Their Length can only go up. They carry a "Career in progress" badge.

    Sources: Basketball Reference, Lahman Baseball Database, NHL Stats API, f1db, Jeff Sackmann's tennis data, Pro Football Reference, Wikipedia.
