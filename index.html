    Reading the map

    Every star is one athlete's whole career. Three things to look at:

    Position comes from the three axes (next section).
    Size and brightness come from the GOAT score. Big and bright means all-time. Small and dim means great, but not in that conversation.

    By default you're seeing the top 125 from each sport, 0 stars total. If you want the full thing, check Show everyone and all 0 athletes in the data show up. It gets crowded.

    The 3 axes

    Quick idea first. Every season, every league has an elite tier, basically the best players that year (around the top 1 in 8 who played enough). All three axes measure you against that group. So a 1965 season gets compared to 1965, not to 2025. That's the only fair way I could find to put Bill Russell's era and Jokić's era on the same map.

    X

    Height

    How good were you at your best?

    Your 3 best seasons, each one measured by how far above that year's elite you were. Then I average them.

    This is the peak. High here means nobody in your era could do what you did those years.

    Y

    Length

    How long did you stay great?

    A straight count of how many seasons you finished inside the elite tier.

    One monster year counts once. Fifteen really good years count fifteen times. Derrick Rose won MVP at 22, then the injuries hit. One elite season on this axis. That one still hurts.

    Z

    Separation

    How far ahead of everyone were you?

    The gap between you and the next best player that season. I take your 2 biggest gaps.

    Being #1 is one thing. Being #1 by a mile is this.

    Heads up: Height and Separation end up pretty close for most people, since the highest peaks usually come with the biggest gaps. Length is where careers really split apart.

    The GOAT score

    The GOAT score mixes the three axes. Right now it's Height %, Length %, Separation %.

    I didn't just pick those numbers. I tried every combination in 5% steps and kept the one that lined up best with the all-time lists people already argue about: ESPN's #NBArank, The Athletic's NHL99 and NFL 100, Joe Posnanski's Baseball 100, FourFourTwo's top 100, and a few more. That mix is the Experts preset.

    You don't have to agree with it. Drag the sliders under Build your GOAT formula and watch the stars resize. The #1 at the top changes too. Try Longevity and see who takes over.

    Scores are scaled so 50 is the average of a sport's top 100 and 99 is the single best career on the map.

    Comparing across sports

    This was the hardest part. Every sport gets one "season value" number, using the best all-around stat I could get for it. That number then goes up against that season's elite:

    NBAWin Shares + VORP, per 82 games
    NHLPoints per 82 games (defensemen scaled up so they're not buried under forwards)
    MLBHitters: OPS+ above average × plate appearances. Pitchers: ERA+ above average × innings
    NFLApproximate Value from Pro Football Reference, per 17 games
    Formula 1Points per race, using today's points system for every era
    TennisWins weighted by how big the tournament was, minus half-weighted losses
    SoccerLeague goals per 34 matches

    What's missing

    I'd rather tell you now than have you find it. NBA, NHL, MLB, F1 and tennis use every player in league history, so the elite tier is legit. NFL and soccer use a smaller group I put together, which is why their cards say lower data confidence.

    • No playoffs. Clutch isn't in here yet. It's the next thing I want to add.
    • Soccer only counts goals. That's rough on defenders, keepers and playmakers. Maldini deserves better.
    • Active players aren't done. Their Length can only go up, so they get a "Career in progress" tag.
    • NFL stops at the 2024 season for now.

    Data: Basketball Reference, the Lahman Baseball Database, the NHL Stats API, f1db, Jeff Sackmann's tennis data, Pro Football Reference, Wikipedia.

    Why I made this

    I started this during the World Cup, watching USA casuals go back and forth with football fans about who the real GOAT of GOATs was. Naturally I'm biased. CR7 is the greatest athlete of any sport (he is). But I wanted to actually back that up instead of just guess.

    Most GOAT debates end up at stats anyway, so I leaned into that. The hard part was making the stats fair across sports that have nothing in common, which is what the whole elite-tier thing is about.

    For the record, the model has CR7 19th overall and 3rd in soccer. I'm still working on that.

    If you leave without being completely pissed off about where your guy landed, or who's missing, I'll call that a win. I love sports, I love numbers, and I like getting into people's heads. This is all three.
